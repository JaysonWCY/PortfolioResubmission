Collaborative Discussion 2: The Pros and cons of logging – The impact of log4j

Discussion Topic Read Berger. (2024) and Nyangaresi, V. O. et al. (2024) and then post your thoughts on the issues of logging for security analysis versus the issues of log-related exploits. You should support your arguments with appropriate academic references.

RESPONSE

Logging allows for the monitoring and auditing of a system to prevent any unwanted actions. The use of logging can help security programs detect any suspicious behaviour, such as repeated failed login attempts, privilege escalation, or access to restricted files. By maintaining detailed logs, administrators are able to audit system activity, trace incidents back to their source, and strengthen their defences against similar attacks in the future. This however, comes with the drawback. In both Berger. (2024) and Nyangaresi, V. O. et al. (2024), they state that logging itself can be used as an exploit. This can come in ways such as using logs to identify vulnerable information or making logging systems themselves run malicious code. There could even be the case of forging or manipulating logs which may cause issues and holes in the security. In conclusion, logging is a useful tool if done safely and in a protected manner but if handled incorrectly, can be a vulnerability itself.

SUMMARY

Other peers also have come to the same conclusion that logs when used in the proper framework can be incredibly useful, but when done wrongly can be taken advantage of. Some even considered the methods of protecting logs such as log encryption as well as proper handling method for logs. Some even consider the issue with how improper handling of logs can even lead to issues regarding privacy standards, such as GDPR (I (Legislative acts) REGULATIONS REGULATION (EU) 2016/679 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL of 27 April 2016 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data, and repealing Directive 95/46/EC (General Data Protection Regulation) (Text with EEA relevance), no date) if sensitive data is leaked.
