Reviewing an Assessment Reporting Template

Read Doroiman, M. M. and Sîrghi, N. (2024) and then post a message that reflects your thoughts and responses to the questions below. You should support your arguments with appropriate academic references. In your responses, consider the following questions:

  • What do you consider as a fully digital enterprise?
  • What are the cyber security challenges/concerns with a fully digital enterprise?
  • What are the cyber security challenges for a bricks and mortar SME wanting to become a digital enterprise?

RESPONSE

The assessment template from PurpleSec provided a detailed description of vulnerabilities as well as their remediation methods. It lacks in NCSC requirements in its lack of details in certain fields. One of the areas of such is the lack of details about configurations such as TLS/HTPS configurations and encryption protocols which are crucial in NCSC. It also doesn’t provide much information about access management practices and policies.

PurpleSec however, excels by providing clear categorisation of its threats into critical, high medium, and low. They also provide very in-depth guidance on remediation tactics. While this is beneficial to the technical staff, it would be overwhelming and confusing at certain times. This is due to the inclusion of too many in-depth data which may not be applicable to all circumstances. The impact of these vulnerabilities could also have been generally explained alongside the risk severities.

REFLECTION

Going in-depth to compare the assessment template with NCSC guidelines proved difficult. This was due to the lack of technical skills to be able to tell what fully falls into which category. While it was easy to point out issues that I was familiar with, such as TLS/HTTPS as well as encryption issues, other fields such as patching or group policies I was unfamiliar with. This would mean that I would be unsure if the template had gone down to enough detail or not. The solution was simply to do as much research on each of the vulnerabilities presented, at least on a general level, enough to understand if the presented information was sufficient or not.
